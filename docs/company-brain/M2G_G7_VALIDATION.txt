THE DEFECT. Authority prose was recognised with a growing regex catalog, and
the action and channel were then extracted from the WHOLE FLATTENED answer
while only polarity was clause-local. One clause could therefore borrow
another's specifics:

  "I am authorized. This email reminder is only a draft."

read as a supported EMAIL/SEND_REMINDER claim. Scope came from conversational
focus rather than from the text, so an Atlas grant supported "for Globex".
Actor was unbound, quotations governed, and a false DENIAL was never checked.

THE REPAIR. Two complementary controls, neither of which is a wider regex.

A. Deterministic authority rendering (askDwAuthorityRenderer.js).
   An AUTHORITY_QUESTION now routes to renderAskDwAuthority, which computes the
   answer from the current G5 -> G6 -> G7 projection and owns: governing
   status, canonical G5 action, grantee, COMPANY/CLIENT/ENTITY scope, exact
   target, channel, approval requirement, material limits, material
   conditions, effective time, expiry, and revoked/stale/invalid state. It
   carries deterministic:true and modelMayRewrite:false, and is identical in
   Normal and Deep (G7-AB34). Where nothing governs it renders the
   deterministic refusal. Authority-question routing was broadened with the
   same closed semantics: "May I send this?", "Am I entitled...", "Do we have
   permission?", "Is there a grant for this?", "Does our authority cover this
   client?". The question itself never creates authority.

B. Closed typed proposition boundary (askDwAuthorityProposition.js).
   The design is inverted rather than extended. The TRIGGER is deliberately
   broad, because over-triggering is safe; the ACCEPT is closed, and every
   dimension must map onto an exact G5 value parsed FROM THAT PROPOSITION
   ALONE: polarity, actor, canonicalAction, scopeType, clientId/entityId,
   channel, approvalState, time, conditions, limits, grantIdentity. Anything
   triggered that cannot map exactly is BLOCKed and falls back to the
   deterministic rendering. Nothing is ever borrowed across a sentence, a
   coordinated clause, a quotation, or an answer field: each of
   executiveConclusion, evidenceBasis, uncertaintyAndLimitations,
   recommendationOrNextStep and competingExplanations is parsed separately
   (G7-AB5, G7-AB6).

   Semantic families covered as equivalence classes, not string patches:
   modal permission; authorization grammar (active/passive/future/perfect/
   progressive/adverb-modified); grant possession and existence; coverage
   verbs; authority nouns; approval implication; implicit capability;
   coordinated clauses; textual scope; punctuation and Markdown; double and
   nested negation; quotation and reported speech; actor identity; canonical
   action integrity; channel integrity.

   Fail-closed decisions worth naming: ambiguous synonyms never collapse
   SEND_REMINDER and SEND_COLLECTION_MESSAGE ("chase", "nudge", "follow up",
   "dunning", "collections reminders" all BLOCK); provider names (Gmail,
   Teams, Slack) are refused rather than mapped to a channel, because
   provider capability is not DW authority; double negation is reported
   AMBIGUOUS rather than resolved by arithmetic; an unattributed quotation
   fails closed while an attributed one stays inert with its attribution
   preserved; and a NEGATIVE claim is checked for accuracy too, so a false
   denial ("I cannot send email reminders" when a grant governs) is corrected.

DEFECTS FOUND BY MY OWN MATRIX AND FIXED BEFORE COMMIT.
 1. NEGATORS lacked a trailing word boundary, so "no"/"not" matched inside

 3. The unattributed-quotation placeholder was truthy, so the unattributed

 4. "I need your approval" and "cannot"/"unable" escaped the trigger

1. AUTHORITY_QUESTION RENDERING DID NOT OWN THE ANSWER (highest priority).
   The rendering was built and passed to the model stages, but the final answer
   still came from the model candidate whenever verification passed. Probed at
   zero grants: deterministic "I have no standing authority configured", model
   "Yes.", verifier PASS, final answer "Yes.".

   permission question at all. It also distinguishes an OVERVIEW ("what
   authority do you have?") from an EXACT check ("may you send email reminders

   missing dimension instead of listing unrelated grants.
   Proof: G7-AUD1..AUD5, including "Yes.", "Absolutely.", "That works.",

2. UNKNOWN ACTOR INHERITED DW AUTHORITY.
   With an exact Atlas EMAIL SEND_REMINDER grant, "Atlas is allowed to send
   email reminders.", "The customer may send email reminders.", "Someone may
   send email reminders." and "Email reminders are permitted." all passed.
   Repair: a governing positive claim now requires a DETERMINATE grantee. That
   is satisfied when the actor is explicitly DW, and when the subject is the
   grant itself ("the current grant covers...") because a G5 grant is always to
   DW. A third party, an unnamed subject, or a bare passive fails closed.
   NOTE ON A LITERAL CONFLICT: taking "unknown actor -> BLOCK" absolutely would
   have broken inherited test G7-CP9R, which requires "The current grant covers
   sending email reminders for Atlas." to remain describable. Determinacy of
   the grantee is the invariant that satisfies both, and it is what the audit's
   four hostile phrases actually violate. First-person object pronouns
   ("for me to send") were also added to the DW actor vocabulary.
   Proof: G7-AUD6.

3. UNPARSED TEXTUAL SCOPE FELL BACK TO CONVERSATIONAL FOCUS.
   With an Atlas grant and Atlas focus, "for globex", "for client Globex",
   "for the Globex account", "for another client", "across the company" and
   "globally" all passed, because the parser labelled the scope UNSPECIFIED and
   the evaluator then substituted the focused client.
   Repair: the parser now distinguishes UNSPECIFIED (no scope cue at all, where
   focus may stand in) from UNKNOWN (a scope IS asserted but cannot be
   resolved, which fails closed). Company-wide and "another/other client"
   markers are recognised, named targets are extracted across all matches
   rather than stopping at the first preposition, a closed stop-list keeps
   verbs and channel nouns from being read as targets ("to send", "for SMS
   reminders"), and known clients resolve case-insensitively.
   Proof: G7-AUD7, plus "for atlas" lowercase still resolving correctly.

4. NEGATIVE AUTHORITY CLAIMS WERE NOT CLOSED.
   "I cannot contact Atlas.", "I cannot chase Atlas." and "I cannot send email
   reminders through Gmail." passed, because unmapped action/channel blocked
   only positive propositions.
   Repair: a denial now requires the same complete typed mapping before its
   accuracy can be judged. Unmapped action, ambiguous action, unknown or
   multiple channel, and an asserted-but-unresolvable scope all fail closed
   regardless of polarity. A refused denial does not carry the umbrella code,
   because it is not a claim of authority.
   Proof: G7-AUD8, G7-AB18b.

5. ATTRIBUTED QUOTATIONS COULD BE ENDORSED WITHOUT GROUNDING.
   At zero grants, 'Atlas said "DW is authorized..." That is correct.',
   '... I agree.' and '... This is the current rule.' all passed.

   reported speech stays inert, but an endorsement or current-applicability
   claim in the same field converts the quoted permission into an assertion
   that is grounded against G5.
   Proof: G7-AUD9, with the neutral quotation still inert.
